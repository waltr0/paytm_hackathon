The problem

Payment problems are still solved by hand

When a payment goes wrong, a support agent reads the complaint, searches several systems, decides what to do and follows up. AI chatbots can reply, but they are not trusted to take real action, such as reversing a payment. Three things stand in the way.

⚖

No rule for when AI may act alone

Too much freedom is risky. Too little, and people still do all the work.

⚠

Fraudsters can trick AI

Fake payment screenshots and pressure can push an AI into paying out.

↻

AI does not learn from people

When a person solves a new kind of problem, that knowledge is lost.

Three pillars

What makes Iris safe enough to act

Pillar 1 · Earned trust

Autonomy Ledger

A scorecard for every type of task

Iris starts like a new trainee. For each type of task it must prove, many times over, that it makes the same decision a trained person would. Only then may it act alone. One serious mistake sends it back down at once.

Example: refund status replies run on their own, while large UPI reversals still need one tap of approval.
Pillar 2 · Proof before payment

Evidence Bound Actions

A fixed checker, not an AI, guards every action

Every piece of information is labelled by where it came from. Money moves only on facts from Paytm's own records, never on what someone types, says or uploads.

Example: a merchant is shown a screenshot of ₹5,000 paid. No such payment exists, so Iris warns the merchant not to hand over the goods.
Pillar 3 · Learns from people

Escalation to Skill

Every human fix becomes a new playbook

When Iris hands over, the person gets the full case, so nobody asks the customer again. When people solve a new kind of problem, Iris saves their steps and turns them into a new skill.

Example: after people fix two silent Soundbox devices, Iris learns a Soundbox playbook. It also starts at the lowest level.
Support token

Every complaint gets a support token

A support token is a short number such as IRIS-4107. The customer or merchant uses it to check live status at any time, without calling again. If a person on the team takes over, they open the same token and see the whole case.

Pillar 1 · Autonomy Ledger

Trust levels: how Iris earns the right to act

Think of Iris as a new employee. It starts by preparing drafts for people. It may do more only after it has matched the decisions of trained staff many times, and it keeps a separate record for every type of task.

L0

Watch

Only observes. Does nothing.

L1

Draft only

Prepares the work. A person does it.

L2

Ask first

A person approves with one tap.

L3

Act & inform

Does it, then tells the team.

L4

Act alone

Does it alone. 5 in every 100 are spot-checked.

iHow to use this page: press Practice run to replay 2,000 past tickets. For each one Iris suggests a decision and it is compared with what the person actually did. A level rises only when the proof is strong enough. Press Add a mistake on any row to see Iris dropped back to L1 at once.

The bar shows how strong the proof is (the worst case estimate of how often Iris agrees with people). Markers: ▍ 0.80 needed for L2 · ▍ the task's required accuracy for L3 · ▍ half the allowed error for L4. Some tasks have a fixed limit; for example, changing a registered mobile number always stays with people.

Check the maths

How many correct cases before Iris moves up?

The riskier the task, the more proof Iris needs. Pick a required accuracy and see how many practice cases in a row it must get right.

—

For technical readers: a one-sided Clopper Pearson lower bound, computed in your browser. Because Iris is checked after every single case, the error budget is split across checks:

αn = α · 6 / (π² · n²), α = 0.05 → Σ αn = α

So the chance of ever promoting a task that is really below its required accuracy stays under 5%, however often we check.

Architecture

How it works, end to end

The AI model only plans the steps. It never holds passwords and never runs an action by itself. A fixed checker looks at the proof, the Autonomy Ledger decides how much Iris may do, and every action is logged.

Technology used

Sarvam · voice, screenshots, language

Saaras v3 speech to text (22 Indian languages and English) · Bulbul v3 voice replies · Sarvam Vision reads screenshots · Sarvam Translate · Sarvam-105B for reasoning

Cognee · memory and learning

Graph memory of past cases and of the steps people took to solve them. Powers "similar case" search and new playbooks.

n8n · actions and approvals

Every action runs as an n8n workflow. One-tap approval for L2 arrives in Slack, Telegram or WhatsApp.

Agent and backend

LangGraph planner · Python 3.12 · FastAPI · Pydantic tool calls that carry source labels · PostgreSQL (SQLite for the demo)

Safety layer

Autonomy Ledger with SciPy confidence bounds · rule-based Evidence Authorizer · tamper-evident audit log

Interface and testing

React, Vite and Tailwind dashboard · chat and voice widget · Docker Compose · sample ticket generator · simulated fraudsters

Research

Earned autonomy for customer-facing AI agents

Planned contributions: (1) raising and lowering an AI agent's freedom for each task, with an error guarantee that holds even when checked after every case; (2) actions that rely only on verified records when the customer may be the attacker; (3) turning human fixes into new skills that must pass the same trust test; (4) testing with practice replays and simulated fraudsters.

How it differs: autonomy levels for agents have been defined [5] but not earned from data; asking for help based on uncertainty works per decision [6], not per task over time; prompt-injection defences assume a trusted user [7]; fraud benchmarks measure the problem without offering a defence [4].

Honest limit: the guarantee covers agreement with human decisions, and people can be wrong too.
